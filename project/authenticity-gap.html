Research / Brand Strategy

The Authenticity Gap

A strategic audit of brand perception versus consumer decision-making logic among Gen Z in India’s D2C fashion sector.

PDF Download Back to Projects
Tools usedQuestionnaire design, survey analysis, Excel, qualitative coding, consumer insight synthesis
MethodologySurvey-led dissertation work combined consumer perception analysis, question-wise interpretation, and thematic synthesis around image, honesty, consistency, and mismatch between promise and experience.
Overview

This dissertation examines the point at which visually strong D2C fashion brands fail to convert attention into trust. The core argument is that authenticity operates as a practical decision gate for Gen Z, not as a decorative brand value.

Business Problem

Many brands invest heavily in image-led communication, influencer presence, and trend language, but those cues do not automatically translate into credibility. The project identifies the resulting gap between perceived brand appeal and purchase confidence.

Objectives
  • Map the criteria Gen Z uses to judge authenticity and brand credibility.
  • Identify the cues that trigger trust and the signals that cause withdrawal.
  • Translate consumer perception patterns into actionable brand strategy.
Results
  • Authenticity emerged as a threshold condition for retention.
  • Trust withdrawal occurred primarily before purchase, not after delivery.
  • Repeated inconsistency led to disengagement and brand switching.
Business Impact

The study offers a practical rule for fashion brands: visual appeal must be supported by consistent proof, or attention is lost before conversion.

Key Learnings

Strong branding is not the same as credible branding. The market rewards coherence between message, product, reviews, and behaviour.
